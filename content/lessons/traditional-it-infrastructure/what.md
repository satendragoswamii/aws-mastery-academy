## The simple version

**Before the cloud, if a company wanted a computer to run its website or its accounts, it had to buy one, put it somewhere, and look after it forever.**

Think about owning a car versus booking a taxi. When you own a car you pay a lot up front, you pay for insurance, parking and servicing even on days you don't drive, and when it breaks down it's your problem. A taxi you pay for only when you ride.

Traditional IT is owning the car. A company:

- **Buys** servers, storage and network equipment, often for lakhs or crores of rupees.
- **Houses** them in a server room or a rented space in a data centre, with power, cooling and security.
- **Runs** them with its own staff: installing software, applying security patches, replacing failed disks at 2 a.m.
- **Replaces** them every three to five years when they get old.

This is called **on-premises** (usually shortened to **on-prem**): the computers are on the company's own premises, or at least under its own control.

## The professional version

**On-premises infrastructure** is compute, storage and network capacity that an organisation procures, owns and operates itself. The organisation is responsible for the entire stack, from the building and power up to the application:

| Layer | Who handles it on-prem |
|---|---|
| Building, power, cooling, physical security | You (or your colocation provider) |
| Racks, servers, storage arrays, network switches | You: purchase, install, maintain, refresh |
| Virtualisation layer and operating systems | You |
| Databases, middleware, runtimes | You |
| Applications and data | You |

Hardware is a **capital expenditure (CapEx)**: bought up front and depreciated over its useful life. Capacity is fixed between purchases, so it has to be **planned** months in advance against a forecast peak.

> **Key idea:** with on-prem you pay for **capacity you own**, whether or not you use it. With the cloud you pay for **capacity you use**, whether or not you own any.

On-prem is not "wrong". Many organisations still run on-prem or **hybrid** environments (you'll meet hybrid cloud two lessons from now). But understanding its costs and delays is the only way to understand why the cloud exists, and that is exactly what the certification exams test.
