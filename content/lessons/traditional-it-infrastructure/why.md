## Why did companies run their own servers?

For decades there was simply **no alternative**. If you needed computing, you needed a computer, and computers had to live somewhere. Owning them also gave companies:

- **Full control** over hardware, software, network and physical access.
- **Predictable location** of data, which mattered for regulations.
- **No dependency** on an outside provider's internet connection or business.

Those reasons are real, and they explain why on-prem is still common in banks, governments, hospitals and factories.

## The problems that pushed companies to the cloud

### 1. You have to guess the future
A server takes weeks to arrive, so you must decide **today** how much you'll need **next year**. Guess too low and your site crashes on its busiest day. Guess too high and money sits idle in a rack. Either way you're wrong, because nobody knows the future.

### 2. Huge up-front cost
Starting a new project means a large purchase before you've earned a single rupee from it. That favours big, established companies and punishes experiments. If the project fails, you still own the hardware.

### 3. Slow
A typical journey for one new server in a large company:

~~~text
Request -> budget approval -> quotes -> purchase order -> vendor delivery
        -> racking and cabling -> OS install -> security hardening -> hand-over
Typical total: 6 to 12 weeks
~~~

Every new idea waits in that queue.

### 4. Mostly idle
Because servers are bought for the **peak**, they spend most of their life far below it. Average utilisation of 10–20% is common. You paid for 100%.

### 5. "Undifferentiated heavy lifting"
Racking servers, swapping disks and patching firmware are hard work, but they don't make your product better than a competitor's. AWS calls this **undifferentiated heavy lifting**: effort every company has to spend that gives none of them an advantage.

### 6. Expensive to be global or resilient
Want a second site in case the first floods? Want low latency for users in Europe? On-prem, each of those is another building, another set of hardware, another team.

## Why this matters for you

Every one of the **six advantages of cloud computing** you'll memorise for the exam is the answer to one of these problems:

| On-prem problem | Cloud advantage (AWS wording) |
|---|---|
| Huge up-front cost | Trade fixed expense for variable expense |
| Low utilisation, high unit cost | Benefit from massive economies of scale |
| Guessing the future | Stop guessing capacity |
| Slow procurement | Increase speed and agility |
| Undifferentiated heavy lifting | Stop spending money running and maintaining data centres |
| Expensive to be global | Go global in minutes |

If you understand the left column, you'll never have to memorise the right one.
