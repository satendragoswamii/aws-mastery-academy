## Why the Well-Architected Framework matters

### "It works" isn't enough
The first version of almost every system works on a good day. Problems appear on bad days: a disk fails, a developer deletes the wrong table, traffic triples during a sale, the bill doubles, nobody knows who owns a resource. The framework's questions are about **bad days**, asked before they happen.

### A shared language
"That's a reliability risk" or "this is a cost-optimization trade-off" means the same thing to developers, architects, managers and AWS. Reviews become faster and less personal: the framework asks the questions, not a colleague.

### It's built from experience
The best practices come from what went wrong (and right) across a huge number of AWS customer workloads. Following them is cheaper than learning each lesson yourself in production.

### Trade-offs become visible
You can't maximise every pillar at once. Multi-Region active-active is great for reliability and expensive. The framework helps you choose **on purpose**: "we accept a 1-hour recovery time to save cost, and we've written that down".

### It pulls together everything you've learned
Every lesson so far maps to a pillar:

| Earlier lesson | Pillar |
|---|---|
| Scalability and elasticity, High availability, Fault tolerance, Disaster recovery, Regions and AZs | Reliability / performance efficiency |
| Cloud security basics, Shared responsibility model | Security |
| Cloud pricing models | Cost optimization |
| Containers, Virtualization (utilisation) | Performance efficiency / sustainability |

## Why this matters for you
CLF-C02 expects you to **name the six pillars** and match practices to them. Associate and professional exams use the pillars as their design vocabulary, and the Solutions Architect exam's domains (secure, resilient, high-performing, cost-optimised) are four of them.
