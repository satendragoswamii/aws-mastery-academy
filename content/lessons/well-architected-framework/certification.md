## Certification relevance

### AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)
**Domain 1: Cloud Concepts, task 1.2: Identify design principles of the AWS Cloud.** You must know the six pillars and match practices to them.

| Question wording | Pillar |
|---|---|
| "run and monitor systems, continually improve processes", "perform operations as code" | **Operational excellence** |
| "protect information and systems", "apply security at all layers", "enable traceability" | **Security** |
| "recover from failure automatically", "scale horizontally", "stop guessing capacity" | **Reliability** |
| "use computing resources efficiently", "go global in minutes", "use serverless architectures" | **Performance efficiency** |
| "avoid unnecessary costs", "adopt a consumption model", "analyse and attribute expenditure" | **Cost optimization** |
| "minimise environmental impact", "maximise utilisation", "use managed services" (for impact) | **Sustainability** |
| "free tool to review workloads against best practices" | **AWS Well-Architected Tool** |
| "best-practice checks for cost, security, fault tolerance, performance, service limits" | **AWS Trusted Advisor** |

### Exam traps
- **There are six pillars**, not five. Sustainability was added in 2021.
- "Design for failure", "multi-AZ", "test recovery" → **reliability**, not performance.
- "Right-sizing" can appear under **cost optimization**; "selecting the right instance type for the workload" under **performance efficiency**. Read the wording: is the goal money or speed?
- The Well-Architected Tool is **free**; Trusted Advisor's full checks depend on your support plan.

### Solutions Architect – Associate (SAA-C03)
Its four domains (secure, resilient, high-performing, cost-optimised architectures) are four of the pillars. Every scenario question is effectively a mini Well-Architected review.
