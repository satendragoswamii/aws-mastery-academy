## The problem the cloud solves

Imagine it's 2005 and you're starting an online shop. Before your first customer arrives, you have to:

1. **Guess** how many customers you'll have, then buy servers big enough for them.
2. **Pay up front**: lakhs of rupees for servers, network equipment and storage.
3. **Wait** weeks for the hardware to be delivered, installed and configured.
4. **Find a room** with enough power, cooling, security and internet capacity.
5. **Hire people** to replace broken disks, apply updates and get up at 3 a.m. when something fails.

Now three things can go wrong:

| What happens | Result without the cloud |
|---|---|
| You guessed **too high** | Expensive servers sit idle. Money wasted. |
| You guessed **too low** and a sale goes viral | The site crashes. Customers leave. Money lost. |
| The idea **fails** | You're stuck owning hardware nobody needs. |

Each of these is a problem of **owning** infrastructure. You pay for capacity whether you use it or not, and you can't change it quickly.

## Why AWS created it

Amazon had this problem itself. Its retail site had to be built for its busiest days, like the holiday season, which left a lot of capacity idle the rest of the year. Over time Amazon got very good at running infrastructure at huge scale, and in 2006 it began **selling that capability** to everyone else as AWS.

## The six advantages AWS describes

AWS summarises the benefits of cloud computing as six advantages. You will see these again in the Cloud Practitioner exam:

1. **Trade fixed expense for variable expense.** Pay for what you use instead of investing heavily up front.
2. **Benefit from massive economies of scale.** AWS buys hardware in enormous volumes, so the cost per unit is lower than you could get alone.
3. **Stop guessing capacity.** Scale up or down within minutes as demand changes.
4. **Increase speed and agility.** New resources are a few clicks or one command away, so teams can experiment cheaply and quickly.
5. **Stop spending money running and maintaining data centres.** Focus on your product, not on racking servers.
6. **Go global in minutes.** Run your application in AWS Regions around the world with very little extra effort.

> **Key idea:** The cloud turns infrastructure from something you **own** into something you **consume**. Almost every AWS service is a variation on that idea.
