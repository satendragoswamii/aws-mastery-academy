## The simple version

**Cloud computing means renting computers that someone else owns and runs, over the internet, and paying only for what you use.**

Think about electricity. You don't build a power station at home. You plug into the grid, use as much as you need, and get a bill for exactly what you used. You never think about turbines or power lines.

Cloud computing does the same for computers:

- Instead of **buying** a server (a powerful computer that runs websites, apps and databases), you **rent** one for minutes or hours.
- Instead of waiting weeks for hardware to arrive, you get it in **seconds**.
- Instead of a big payment up front, you get a **monthly bill** for what you actually used.

When you use Gmail, Netflix or Google Drive, the software and your data aren't on your laptop. They run on computers in large buildings called data centres. You reach them over the internet. That's "the cloud": **other people's computers, rented to you on demand.**

## The professional version

The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), in publication SP 800-145, defines cloud computing as:

> a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction.

In practice that means:

- **Computing resources**: processing power, memory, storage, databases and networking.
- **Provisioned**: created and handed to you when you ask.
- **Released**: given back when you're done, so you stop paying.
- **Minimal provider interaction**: you don't phone anyone or sign a purchase order. You click a button or run a command, and software does the rest.

**Amazon Web Services (AWS)** is the largest public cloud provider. It started offering cloud services in 2006 and now offers hundreds of them, from simple file storage (Amazon S3) to virtual servers (Amazon EC2) to machine learning.

> **Remember:** The cloud is not magic and not "somewhere in the sky". It's real computers in real buildings. What changes is **who owns them**, **how fast you get them**, and **how you pay for them**.
